# Discovery: The "To Sell" Pile

## Challenge

People who own clothes they no longer wear **intend** to sell them second-hand,
but the clothes stay in a pile for weeks or months and never get listed.

The challenge is platform-neutral: Vinted, Kleinanzeigen, eBay, flea markets,
and other channels all count.

## Scope

**One journey: from "I should sell this" to "it's listed".**

- In scope: deciding to sell, sorting, taking photos, writing the description,
  pricing, publishing.
- Out of scope for now: buyers, haggling, shipping, payment.
- If the evidence shows that the real drop-off happens after listing, the scope
  will be revisited.

## Evidence Rules

- Participants are labelled P0, P1, P2, and so on. No names or identifying
  details.
- P0 is the project owner's own experience: self-evidence, possibly biased.
- Raw notes go in the evidence log. Interpretation belongs in the synthesis
  sections, not here.
- Missing evidence is listed as an open question, never filled in with a guess.

## Interview Guide

Ask about past behaviour, not opinions about future products. Do not ask
"Would you use an app that...?"

1. Do you have clothes you don't wear anymore? Roughly how many?
2. Tell me about the last time you sold, or tried to sell, a piece of clothing.
   What happened, step by step?
3. Where did you stop or put it off? What was going on at that moment?
4. What's the most annoying part? What's the easiest part?
5. How do you decide on a price?
6. Is there anything you've never tried to sell even though you don't wear it?
   Why not?
7. What do you do instead (give away, donate, keep)?
8. If they're willing: can you show me a listing you started, or photos you
   took for it?

### Self-evidence (P0, possibly biased)

- **Context:** project owner; has a pile of clothes to sell; has sold online
  before; recently went through a major life change.
- **Date and format:** 2026-10-16, self-reported recall (not observed live).
- **What they said, step by step:**
  - First has to look for the clothes they want to sell.
  - Googles the item to see what other people are selling it for.
  - If the lighting isn't good (already dark), waits until the next day to
    take pictures.
  - Uploads the photos and writes a text.
  - "At least 15 minutes for just one piece of clothing."
  - Keeps asking themselves: "Is it worth it? Is it worth it?"
- **What happened last time (during a major life change):**
  - Put some items online and couldn't sell them.
  - Then donated everything they weren't wearing: "I couldn't be bothered to
    put everything online."
  - "It didn't matter how expensive the clothing was, I just donated it because
    I was so tired of it."
- **Where they stopped or put it off:**
  - Waiting for daylight to take photos (delay of a day).
  - Gave up entirely after unsold listings during the life change and donated instead.
- **Limits:** one person, recalled rather than observed; the project owner
  already had a solution idea before discovery, which may colour the account.

#### Pain points by journey phase

- **Decide: is it worth it? (4 sources).** Low expected value stops people
  starting (30% Post Office, 25% Samsung). Forum posters report everyday items
  selling for "about 10% of what you paid" or "£2-4 per item"
  ([Mumsnet thread](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/_chat/5153943-is-selling-clothes-online-worth-it),
  2024). 42% don't know resale value (Vinted/GWI).
- **Sort (2 sources).** One blogger avoided making piles for later because "it
  probably just wouldn't get done at all"
  ([Frugal Family](https://www.frugalfamily.co.uk/decluttering-my-wardrobe-vinted-update/),
  2026-04). The donation-bag figure points the same way.
- **Photograph (3 sources).** 39% gave up over photos (Samsung). Forum posters
  name ironing, photographing and cropping or editing as the big time cost: a
  few hours for about a £5 sale
  ([Mumsnet](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/_chat/5351161-vinted)).
- **Describe (1 source, buyer side).** Unclear descriptions and poor pictures
  are top buyer turn-offs (Samsung). Link to seller anxiety not shown directly.
- **Price (2 sources).** Pricing uncertainty only indirect (42% don't know
  value). Sellers price low "with the intention of it actually selling quickly"
  (Frugal Family).
- **Overall effort and motivation (4 sources).** "Too much effort" 31% and
  motivation 26% (Post Office); a fifth (Samsung); "a lot of effort for hardly
  any reward" (Mumsnet, about 8 posters); "a high-efficiency time-suck"
  ([Substack column](https://leahmclaren.substack.com/p/skinted-and-vinted)).
- **After listing, as a reason not to start (4+ sources, forum-stated, not
  survey-measured).** Lowball offers (Mumsnet, 4-5 posters; Frugal Family);
  Kleinanzeigen ghosting and no-shows (reported YouGov figure of 69% via
  [t3n](https://t3n.de/news/?p=1717170), not verified at the primary source;
  [mobiFlip](https://www.mobiflip.de/mein-tipp-der-woche-kleinanzeigen/));
  pressure of the 3-day dispatch rule (Mumsnet, about 5 posters); Vinted
  Trustpilot reviews repeatedly report buyers winning disputes.

#### Workarounds people mention

- **Do it all in one session and decide on every item immediately**: list it or
  put it in the charity bag, no "later" pile (Frugal Family: 27 items in an
  afternoon).
- **A minimal photo set**: front, back, care label, flaws. About 15 items in
  under an hour ([The Travel Hack](https://thetravelhack.com/blog/tips-for-selling-on-vinted/)).
- **A few listings a day** instead of a big batch (Mumsnet).
- **Price low, counter lowballs at about 75%**, and donate anything still
  unsold after 2 weeks (Frugal Family).
- **Reduce postage friction**: free collection, lockers, pre-bought mailers
  (Mumsnet).
- **Avoid selling**: donate or use a consignment service (Mumsnet).

#### Looked for but not found

- **Reddit** (r/vinted, r/declutter, r/femalefashionadvice, r/de): access
  blocked. No Reddit evidence is included.
- App store reviews about the listing flow itself: none found. Complaints
  mostly concern bans, support, and disputes.
- Seller-barrier data in ThredUp or Vinted reports: none. Their reports cover
  buying or unworn value.
- Studies of procrastination in reselling: none found.
- German survey data on why people don't sell clothes: none found.

## Open Questions

- Where in the journey do people actually stop: before listing or after?
- Does the platform used change where people get stuck?
- Is the barrier per item ("is this worth it?") or across the whole pile
  (energy running out)? P0 donated expensive items too.
- No German evidence on why people don't sell clothes.
- No interviews with other people yet; all direct evidence is P0.
- Was the give-up triggered by the life change (a confound)?
- Was it triggered by the unsold listings rather than by the listing effort?
- What is the motivation for selling: money, sustainability, space?
- At which steps does the "Is it worth it?" doubt appear? Partly answered:
  P0 places it first at the price check (phase 2). Unknown for other people.
- P0 already checks prices early and still doubts the payoff. Is price
  information the missing piece, or something else?

### Persona: The Intending Seller

_A working model of a user group, not a real person. Built from P0 and desk
research only._

- **Who:** someone with clothes they no longer wear who intends to sell them
  second-hand and has sold online before. (Self-reported, P0)
- **Context:** the clothes are in a pile; each item is handled one at a time.
  (Self-reported, P0)
- **Goal:** get some money back for clothes they no longer wear. (Assumption:
  P0 did not state the motivation for selling. To confirm.)
- **Behaviours:**
  - Finds the item, Googles what others sell it for, waits for good light,
    uploads, writes a description. (Self-reported, P0)
  - Gave up and donated during a major life change after some items didn't sell.
    (Self-reported, P0)
- **Pain points:**
  - At least 15 minutes per item. (Self-reported, P0)
  - Repeated doubt about whether it's worth it. (Self-reported, P0; Desk
    research)
  - Photo quality and lighting. (Self-reported, P0; Desk research)
- **Open questions:** see the Open Questions section above.

### Journey Map

**Actor:** the Intending Seller. **Scenario:** a pile of clothes to sell.
**Goal:** get items listed. **Scope:** "I should sell this" → "it's listed",
plus the give-up branch, because it is where the journey ended for P0.

**Across the whole journey:** P0 "keeps asking" "Is it worth it?"
(Self-reported). P0 first places the question at phase 2, the price check.

Thoughts in phases 1-4 were added by P0 in their own words when reviewing the
draft on 2026-10-16 (Self-reported, unprompted).

| Phase | Action | Thought | Feeling | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Pick an item | Looks through the pile for clothes to sell | "Hmm, I always wanted to sell this." | Motivated (Self-reported) | Self-reported, P0 |
| 2. Check the price | Googles what others sell the same item for | "Let me check the price to see if it's worth it to sell." | Curious, but starting to get annoyed (Self-reported) | Self-reported, P0 |
| 3. Photograph | Waits until the next day if the light is bad | "Ugh, I have to take good pics, but it's already dark and I want to use daylight." | Delayed; the item goes back to "later" (Interpretation, confirmed by P0*) | Self-reported, P0; 39% gave up over photos (Desk research, UK) |
| 4. Upload and describe | Uploads photos, writes the text | "I am already annoyed." | Tired by effort per item (Interpretation, confirmed by P0*) | Self-reported, P0: 15+ min per item |
| 5. Wait _(outside current scope; noted)_ | Some listings don't sell | (not recorded) | Discouraged (Assumption, confirmed by P0*) | Self-reported, P0 |
| 6. Give up | Donates everything during a major life change | "I couldn't be bothered" | "So tired of it" | Self-reported, P0 |

\* Confirmed by P0 when reviewing the draft on 2026-10-16. Agreeing with a
suggested feeling is weaker evidence than naming it unprompted.

**Emotional arc (Self-reported, P0):** motivated → curious but annoyed →
delayed → tired → discouraged → gave up. The annoyance begins at phase 2, the
first step that costs effort, before any photo is taken.

**Pain points:**
- **Phases 1-4:** the full effort is paid per item while the payoff stays
  uncertain. (Interpretation, from P0 and desk research)
- **Phase 3:** lighting turns one listing into a two-day task.
  (Self-reported, P0)
- **Phase 6:** the decision to stop was made for the whole pile at once,
  regardless of value. (Self-reported, P0)

**Opportunities (needs, not solutions):**
- Make the effort of selling feel proportionate to what the person gets back.
- Make progress through a pile feel achievable rather than item-by-item.
- Help the person decide what to do with an item before their energy runs out.

### Point of View

> A person with a pile of clothes they intend to sell **needs selling to feel
> worth the effort it takes**, because each item costs them at least 15 minutes
> while they keep doubting the payoff, and in P0's case the whole pile ended up
> donated, even valuable pieces.

(Interpretation. Hypothesis from one self-report plus desk research.)
